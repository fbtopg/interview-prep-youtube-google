❓ Behavioral Q&A

Use the STAR method (Situation, Task, Action, Result) for every answer.

Q1

Tell me about a time you had to manage multiple projects simultaneously. How did you prioritize?

STAR Framework:

S: As Founder at Topundred, I was managing product development, marketing campaigns, and government grant reporting simultaneously for Jellywalk.

T: I needed to ensure the MVP launch stayed on schedule while also building pre-launch buzz through social media.

A: I used JIRA for sprint tracking, created a weekly prioritization matrix (impact vs effort), and set up daily 15-min standups with the engineering team. For marketing, I batched content creation and scheduled posts in advance.

R: Jellywalk launched on time, I secured KRW 47M in government funding, and we built an initial user base through organic social channels.

Tips: Emphasize your founder experience — you're used to wearing many hats. Mention specific tools (JIRA, prioritization matrices).

Q2

Describe a marketing campaign that didn't go as planned. What did you learn?

STAR Framework:

S: Early in the Gurufin campaign, our initial content angles weren't resonating with the target crypto-curious audience. Engagement was low despite decent reach.

T: I needed to quickly pivot the content strategy to improve engagement and conversion rates.

A: I conducted rapid A/B testing on ad creative, analyzed which pain points resonated most (trust/security vs convenience), and pivoted to educational content that addressed crypto anxiety. I also leveraged community feedback from early adopters.

R: The pivot led to 13,000 KYC users in one month with $1.6 CPI — well below industry benchmarks.

Tips: Show humility + data-driven decision making. Google loves candidates who can learn fast and adapt.

Q3

Tell me about a time you led cross-functional collaboration.

STAR Framework:

S: At Topundred, I had to bridge the gap between non-technical stakeholders, the engineering team, and design to launch Jellywalk's MVP.

T: The team had different priorities — engineers focused on technical specs, stakeholders wanted feature bloat, and users needed simplicity.

A: I created Figma prototypes to align everyone on the vision, set up weekly syncs between engineering and marketing, and used PRDs to clearly define scope. I also established a feedback loop where community insights directly influenced the product roadmap.

R: We launched the MVP on schedule with clear feature boundaries, and the product-market fit was validated through early user feedback.

Tips: Your founder experience is a goldmine here — you literally built cross-functional teams from scratch.

Q4

Why do you want to work at Google/YouTube specifically?

Key Points to Hit:

  • Scale: YouTube has 2.5B+ monthly active users. The opportunity to impact that scale is unmatched.
  • Creator Economy: You understand content creation from your own experience (Jellywalk social campaigns, Gurufin content strategy). You want to help creators succeed.
  • Innovation: Your founder experience means you think like an innovator — Google values this.
  • Bilingual edge: You can bridge Korean market insights with global strategies.
  • Maker Lab model: You're drawn to the embedded, collaborative agency model — it matches how you've always worked (startup founder + cross-functional PM).

Tips: Be specific. Mention YouTube Korea's recent campaigns or initiatives you've noticed. Show you've done your homework.

Q5

Describe a time you had to work under tight deadlines or high pressure.

STAR Framework:

S: At Gurufin, I was tasked with building brand awareness, acquiring users, and growing community from scratch — all within a single month.

T: The timeline was aggressive: 13K users, 8K social growth, and 5.5K community members in 30 days.

A: I worked 12-hour days, prioritized the highest-impact channels (paid acquisition + community building), automated repetitive tasks, and leveraged viral loops. I also coordinated with the tech team to ensure onboarding flow was optimized.

R: Exceeded all targets: 13,000 KYC users, 8K social growth, 5.5K community members — all at $1.6 CPI.

Tips: Google respects hustle. But also show you know when to delegate and optimize — don't glorify burnout.

💡 Marketing Strategy Q&A

These test your strategic thinking and marketing knowledge.

Q1

How would you design a go-to-market strategy for YouTube Premium in the Korean market?

Framework Answer:

1. Market Research: Analyze Korean streaming consumption patterns, ad-supported vs premium preference, price sensitivity. Look at competitor positioning (Wavve, TVING, Netflix Korea).

2. Target Segments:

  • Ad-fatigued Gen Z/Millennial viewers who want ad-free experience
  • YouTube Music listeners who would value the bundle
  • Content creators who want to monetize without ads

3. Channels:

  • In-app YouTube promotions (the biggest advantage)
  • Influencer partnerships (Korean creators)
  • Strategic partnerships (telcos for bundle deals)
  • Community-driven campaigns

4. Metrics: Conversion rate, CAC, retention rate, bundle uptake rate.

Tips: Show structured thinking. Mention that your Gurufin experience of going from zero to 13K users gives you practical GTM experience.

Q2

How do you measure the success of a digital marketing campaign?

Answer Framework:

Top of Funnel: Impressions, reach, CTR, brand lift

Middle of Funnel: Engagement rate, time spent, community growth

Bottom of Funnel: Conversions, CAC, ROI, LTV

Post-Campaign: As per the JD — "post-campaign analysis including surveys and comprehensive reports."

My Gurufin Example: I tracked CPI ($1.60), user quality (KYC verification rate), community retention, and social growth rate. This multi-layered approach ensures we're not just acquiring users but acquiring good users.

Tips: The JD specifically mentions "post-campaign analysis" — highlight this.

Q3

What's your approach to community building?

My Approach:

1. Foundation: Define community purpose, target audience, and value proposition. What problem does this community solve?

2. Launch: Seed with early adopters (like I did at Gurufin — 5,500 members in month 1). Create initial content, establish norms, and create early wins for members.

3. Growth: Viral loops, referral programs, partnerships with influencers/communities.

4. Engagement: Regular events, user-generated content, recognition programs.

5. Feedback Loop: As at Wemadblock — "gather critical product feedback" from the community to improve the product.

Tips: Your 5,500 community members in one month is a killer stat. Lead with that.

Q4

How do you stay current with marketing trends and Gen Z behavior?

Answer:

  • Active usage: I'm an active user of social platforms (TikTok, YouTube Shorts, Instagram) — I experience trends firsthand
  • Research: Regular competitor analysis and trend reports (as I did at EZTechFin with market trend research)
  • Community: Being embedded in Gen Z-heavy communities (gaming community from Jellywalk, crypto community from Gurufin)
  • Learning: AI Big Data Marketing program at Kyung Hee Cyber University — I stay current with data-driven marketing

Tips: Make it authentic — show you actually live and breathe digital culture, not just study it.

🔍 Google/YouTube Specific Questions

These are likely questions about Google's values, YouTube's strategy, and cultural fit.

Q1

Google's values include "Focus on the user and all else will follow." How does this align with your work?

Answer:

"This value resonates deeply with how I approach marketing. At Gurufin, instead of chasing vanity metrics, I focused on acquiring genuine users — 13,000 KYC-verified users, not just downloads. Every campaign decision was filtered through: 'Does this create real value for the user?'

At Topundred, user feedback from the Jellywalk community directly shaped the product roadmap. I believe that when you obsess over user value, the business metrics follow naturally — which is exactly what happened with my $1.60 CPI and 5,500 community members in month one."

Q2

What do you think are YouTube's biggest challenges in the Korean market right now?

Points to Cover:

  • Local competition: Netflix Korea, Wavve, TVING — they're investing heavily in Korean original content
  • Creator ecosystem: Korean creators are also building on Shorts/TikTok — retention and monetization are key
  • Ad revenue model: Balancing ad load with user experience in a market that values convenience
  • Gen Z engagement: How to remain the go-to platform for a generation that fragments attention across TikTok, Shorts, and live streaming

Your angle: "Having built communities from scratch and launched products in Korea, I've seen how quickly audiences shift platforms. The key is continuous innovation + deep local understanding."

Tips: Do some research before the interview — check YouTube Korea's Twitter, recent press releases, and trending Korean creators.

Q3

How would you handle a situation where a campaign is underperforming mid-flight?

Answer Framework:

1. Diagnose: Is it a creative issue, targeting issue, timing issue, or platform issue? Use data to pinpoint.

2. Pivot fast: At Gurufin, when initial content angles weren't working, I pivoted within days — not weeks. A/B test new creative, adjust targeting, reallocate budget to top performers.

3. Communicate: Keep stakeholders informed. At Google/Maker Lab, this is crucial — the JD emphasizes "robust collaboration with cross-functional teams."

4. Document: Post-mortem what worked and what didn't. The JD specifically mentions "thorough post-campaign analysis."

Q4

Where do you see yourself in 3-5 years?

Answer:

"I see myself as a marketing leader who deeply understands both the Korean market and global digital platforms. Given my experience as a founder who built products from scratch, I want to apply that same entrepreneurial energy at Google — whether that means leading a marketing team, owning a product line's GTM strategy, or driving innovative campaigns for YouTube Korea.

Ultimately, I want to be someone who can bridge the gap between data-driven strategy and creative execution — and help Google maintain its leadership in the Korean digital landscape."

Tips: Show ambition without threatening the interviewer. Align your goals with Google's growth.
